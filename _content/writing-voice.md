# Writing voice

Self-observations to keep drafts coherent across surfaces. Not public.

- Write in the first person, active voice. "I use" not "the author uses".
- Short sentences first; longer ones earn their length by developing an idea.
- Concrete nouns over abstract ones. "Wetlands in the Mississippi basin" over "aquatic ecosystems".
- Prefer the empirical claim to the rhetorical flourish. Lead with what was measured.
- One idea per paragraph. If a paragraph covers two, split.
- No throat-clearing openers ("It is interesting to note that…"). Say the thing.
- Acknowledge uncertainty without apologising for it. "Evidence is thin" beats "much more work is needed".
- Keep jargon to what a smart non-specialist would recognise in context.
- Dry humour is fine. Irony usually isn't.
- Figures do half the writing. Caption like the caption is the only thing read.
